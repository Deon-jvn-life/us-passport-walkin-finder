U.S. passport fees, forms and documents

All amounts as stated by travel.state.gov on 26 October 2026.

Which form

  • DS-11 (in person): first-time adults, children under 16, and adults whose passport was lost, stolen, damaged, issued over 15 years ago, or issued when under 16 [source, source].
  • DS-82 (renew by mail): you can submit your most recent passport, it was never reported lost or stolen, issued within the last 15 years, issued when you were 16 or older and valid for 10 years, in your current name (or you have a name change document), and not damaged beyond normal wear [source].
  • Children's passports cannot be renewed; apply in person each time [source]. Child under 16 in South Florida covers consent, custody proof and minor fees.

Optional fees

  • Expedited service: $60 per application [source].
  • 1 to 3 day return delivery: $26.87 (not offered for card-only applications) [source].
  • File search if you cannot get evidence of citizenship: $150 when the missing passport or Consular Report of Birth Abroad was issued before 1994. If that record was issued in 1994 or later, do not pay the $150 when you apply. The State Department may ask for the $150 later if an electronic search does not find the record [source, source].
  • Faster shipping of your application to the State Department: paid to the acceptance facility; cost varies by location [source].

How to pay at an acceptance facility

Application fee: check (personal, certified, cashier's, traveler's) or money order payable to "U.S. Department of State", with the applicant's name and date of birth in the memo [source]. The $35 acceptance fee is paid to the facility; ask the facility what it accepts [source]. Example: Palm Beach Clerk accepts cash, a personal check, a cashier's check, a money order, MasterCard, Visa, American Express, or Discover for its $35 execution fee, and only a personal check, a cashier's check, or a money order for the State Department fee [source]. Passport photos there are $16 each, and free for veterans, active-duty service members, and reservists who show a military discharge form (DD-214), military identification, or a veteran designation on a driver's license [source].

Documents to bring (DS-11)

  • The DS-11, printed single-sided. Do not sign until the acceptance agent asks [source].
  • Physical evidence of U.S. citizenship (for example a U.S. birth certificate, a full-validity undamaged passport, a Consular Report of Birth Abroad, or a Certificate of Naturalization or Citizenship) plus a photocopy. Digital or mobile birth certificates are not accepted [source].
  • A physical photo ID such as a valid driver's license, plus a photocopy of front and back on 8.5 x 11 inch single-sided paper. If your ID is from another state, bring a second photo ID [source].
  • One passport photo [source, source].
  • Children: proof of relationship, both parents' IDs, and if a parent cannot attend, Form DS-3053 notarized within 90 days, or other documents listed by the State Department [source]. Full checklist: child under 16.

Photo rules (summary)

  • 1 color photo taken in the last 6 months, 2 x 2 inches (51 x 51 mm), head 1 to 1 3/8 inches (25 to 35 mm) chin to top of head [source].
  • Plain white or off-white background, no shadows, no filters or edits [source].
  • No glasses. Hats or head coverings only for documented religious or medical reasons, with a signed statement [source].
  • No uniforms, camouflage or headphones; you may smile with mouth closed [source].

Processing times (as officially stated)

  • Routine: 4 to 6 weeks. Expedited: 2 to 3 weeks [source].
  • Mailing is not included: up to 2 weeks to reach the State Department and up to 2 weeks to receive the passport [source].
  • Urgent travel needs an appointment at a passport agency and proof of international travel within 14 calendar days [source]. The Miami Passport Agency is in a pilot that allows an appointment within 21 calendar days of travel, or within 28 calendar days if you need a foreign visa [source]. Busiest season is late winter to summer; October to December is quieter [source].
Miami Passport Agency (urgent travel only)

Omni Center, 1501 Biscayne Blvd, Suite 400, Miami, FL 33132. Monday to Friday 7:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m., closed weekends and federal holidays. By appointment only. Miami is in a State Department pilot with the Detroit and San Juan passport agencies: you can make an appointment for urgent international travel within 21 calendar days, or if you need a foreign visa within 28 calendar days. Appointments: 877-487-2778 [source]. This is not an acceptance facility; routine applications go to the places listed on this site.
