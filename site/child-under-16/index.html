Passport for a child under 16 in South Florida

In short. A child under 16 applies in person with Form DS-11. Bring the child. Both parents or guardians go, or you bring the consent or custody documents on this page. You cannot renew a child's passport, and the passport is valid for 5 years.

A child under 16 applies in person with Form DS-11 at a passport acceptance facility. Bring the child. You cannot renew a child's passport, and the passport is valid for 5 years. Both parents or guardians go with the child, or you bring the consent or custody documents below. [source]

Ages 16 and 17 use different rules. They still apply in person on DS-11, but only one parent or guardian has to show they know about the application, the passport is valid for 10 years, and the fees are the adult fees. Those rules are at the end of this page. [source]

Where to apply in Miami-Dade, Broward and Palm Beach

Use an acceptance facility (post office, clerk of court, or library) if international travel is more than 3 weeks away, and ask whether that office needs an appointment. If the child is traveling internationally in less than 3 weeks, make an appointment at a passport agency once you are within 14 calendar days of travel. [source] The Miami Passport Agency is in a pilot that allows an appointment within 21 calendar days of urgent travel, or within 28 calendar days if you need a foreign visa. [source] Booking steps, proof of travel, and the life-or-death rule. Facilities: Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach.

Miami-Dade Public Library

North Dade Regional, West Kendall Regional and Naranja Branch take applications by appointment only. Book a separate appointment for each person, including each child. A family of five needs five appointments. These libraries do not take passport photos, so bring the photo. The library's execution fee is $35 per application, paid in cash, by cashier's check, or by money order payable to "Miami-Dade Public Library System." The State Department fee is a check or money order only. [source]

On the library's own list, applicants age 15 or under who submit a naturalization certificate or a Certificate of Citizenship must also include a certified birth certificate. [source]

Broward Clerk of Courts

The child must appear. The clerk's office says both parents must appear if both are listed on the birth certificate. If one cannot, bring Form DS-3053 and a copy of that parent's identification, front and back. If a parent is not listed on the legal birth certificate, the clerk's office says only the listed parents must appear or give consent in writing. [source]

Pay the State Department fees (application, expedite, and 1 to 3 day delivery) by personal check, cashier's check, or money order to "U.S. Department of State." The clerk says each applicant needs a separate money order, and that a check or money order must be for the exact amount. The clerk's own fees are $35 to execute the application, $15 for a photo, and $1 per page for copies, paid in cash or by American Express, Discover, MasterCard, or Visa. [source]

Form DS-11

  • Use Form DS-11. Complete it in the State Department form filler and print it. Download the PDF if the form filler fails. [source, source]
  • Print single-sided, on 8.5 by 11 inch paper, in vertical portrait format, with the form filling the page. Double-sided or horizontal printouts are not accepted. [source, source]
  • Do not sign until the acceptance agent asks. The agent checks identification, gives an oath, and then has you sign the child's application. [source]
  • If you use the form filler, the only handwriting on the form should be the original signature and the date. [source]
  • Broward Clerk tells applicants to complete DS-11 in black ink only, with no white-out. [source]
  • Do not staple the photo. The agent reviews it and staples it. [source]
  • You can request a passport book, a passport card, or both. If the child travels internationally several times a year, check the large book box for extra visa pages. [source]

Both parents or guardians

Both parents or guardians must be present with the child, unless you have the documents in the next sections. Each parent or guardian who appears brings a physical photo ID. The usual ID is a fully valid driver's license or enhanced driver's license. If the ID is from a different state than the one where you are applying, bring a second photo ID. Photocopy the front and back of each parent's or guardian's ID on one side of 8.5 by 11 inch paper. [source]

One parent cannot attend, and both still have custody

The parent who cannot come signs and dates Form DS-3053, Statement of Consent, in front of a certified notary. Submit that form with the DS-11, plus a photocopy of the photo ID shown to the notary. DS-3053 and other notarized statements must be submitted within 90 days after they are signed or notarized. [source, source]

  • An electronically notarized statement is accepted if state law allows that notarization. Bring a printed copy. [source]
  • If that parent is outside the United States, some countries require DS-3053 to be notarized at a U.S. embassy or consulate, not by a local notary. The State Department list currently includes Haiti, the Dominican Republic, Jamaica, Venezuela, Honduras, Nicaragua, and others. Use the list on the child passport page before you rely on a local notary. [source]

Sole custody, or you are the only parent

Provide one of these documents: [source]

  • A court order granting you sole custody, or giving only you permission to apply for the child's passport.
  • A certified copy of the child's birth certificate or adoption decree that lists you as the only parent or guardian.
  • A certified copy of the death certificate of the parent or guardian who cannot appear.
  • A certified copy of a judicial declaration of incompetence of the parent who cannot appear.

Foster care or a pending adoption

If the child is pending adoption or in foster care, and a state children services office, a private institution, or an individual is the legal guardian, bring all of the following: [source]

  • A certified copy of the court order naming that office, institution, or individual as the child's legal guardian. The order must say there are no restrictions on issuing a U.S. passport, and it must still be valid, with no pending or upcoming action.
  • A letter on official letterhead from the office, institution, or guardian that lets the person applying with the child sign on their behalf, plus a completed Form DS-3053.
  • The employee ID of the person who wrote the letter, and the employee ID of the person applying with the child, to show both work for that office or institution.

You cannot find the other parent

If both parents or guardians still have custody and you cannot locate the other one, submit Form DS-5525, Statement of Special Family Circumstances, with the DS-11. The State Department may ask for more evidence, such as a custody order, an incarceration order, or a restraining order to protect against international parental child abduction. If the other parent is deployed with the military, that parent should provide a notarized DS-3053 in most cases. [source, source]

Neither parent can appear

The adult who brings the child (for example a grandparent) must show permission: Form DS-3053, or a notarized statement from both parents, submitted within 90 days after notarization, plus a photocopy of both parents' or guardians' photo IDs. If the statement is from only one parent or guardian, also show that parent or guardian has sole custody. [source]

Citizenship evidence

Bring one primary citizenship document on paper, plus a photocopy of the front. Copy the back as well if it has printing. Use white 8.5 by 11 inch paper, one side only. Digital or mobile birth certificates are not accepted. Bring the original, or a replacement that has the seal or stamp of the office that issued it. You may also submit a second certified copy, which the State Department keeps. Leaving out the photocopy can slow the application. [source, source]

The child application page lists these primary documents: [source]

  • A U.S. birth certificate.
  • A Consular Report of Birth Abroad, or another birth document issued by the U.S. Department of State.
  • A Certificate of Citizenship.
  • A full validity, undamaged U.S. passport. For a child under 16, full validity means the passport is or was valid for 5 years.

A U.S. birth certificate must show the child's full name, date of birth, and place of birth; the parents' full names; the city, county, or state of birth; the registrar's signature; the date it was filed with the registrar, which must be within 1 year of birth; and the seal or stamp of the office that issued it. [source]

If the child was born outside the United States, a Certificate of Naturalization is also primary evidence. [source]

Child born abroad who got U.S. citizenship at birth through a U.S. citizen parent: submit the foreign birth certificate listing the parent or parents, evidence of that parent's U.S. citizenship, the parents' marriage certificate if they were married, and a statement of when and where the parents lived in the United States and abroad before the birth. [source]

Citizenship through a parent's naturalization, or through the Child Citizenship Act of 2000, uses a separate list on that same page: the foreign birth certificate, the parent's U.S. citizenship evidence, proof of the child's permanent residence, legal-custody documents, and proof the child lived in the United States with the U.S. citizen parent. Follow that list rather than the birth-certificate list above. [source]

A document in a foreign language needs a professional English translation. The translator must provide a notarized letter about the accuracy of the translation and their ability to translate. [source]

If you cannot get primary evidence, the citizenship page explains secondary evidence: a delayed birth certificate or a letter of no record, plus early public records. [source]

Proof of the parental relationship

The State Department must see the legal relationship between the child and each parent or guardian. A U.S. birth certificate that proves citizenship and names the parents does both jobs. If you do not have that, submit a document that shows who the parents or legal guardians are. Examples the State Department gives: a foreign birth certificate, an adoption decree, a divorce or custody decree, or a court order. [source]

If your name now is different from the name on that document, bring proof of the legal name change, such as a name change decree or a marriage certificate. [source]

Fees for a child under 16

You pay two fees: the application fee to the U.S. Department of State, and a $35 acceptance fee to the facility. Amounts below are the child-under-16 fees on travel.state.gov. [source, source]

DocumentFormApplication feeFacility acceptance fee
Passport bookDS-11$100$35
Passport cardDS-11$15$35
Book and cardDS-11$115$35
  • Pay the application fee by check (personal, certified, cashier's, or traveler's) or money order payable to "U.S. Department of State." Write the child's name and date of birth in the memo. [source, source]
  • The State Department prefers a separate check or money order for each child. One check for a whole family is accepted if the memo lists each applicant's name and date of birth, and how many applications the check covers. [source]
  • At a Broward Clerk office, follow that office's rule: a separate money order for each applicant. [source]
  • Pay the $35 acceptance fee to the facility and ask what it takes. Miami-Dade libraries and the Broward Clerk publish their own methods in the sections above. [source, source, source]
  • Optional expedited service is $60 per application, added to the State Department payment. [source]
  • Optional 1 to 3 day delivery, after the State Department mails the new passport, is $26.87. It is not offered for a card-only application. Do not send a prepaid return envelope. [source]
  • You can pay the acceptance facility to ship the application faster by Priority Mail Express. That price depends on the location. [source, source]
  • The application fee and the execution fee are not refunded, even if no passport is issued. [source]

Routine service is 4 to 6 weeks. Expedited service is 2 to 3 weeks. Those times do not include mailing: up to 2 weeks for the application to reach the State Department, and up to 2 weeks to receive the passport. [source]

Supporting documents (the previous passport, birth certificate, or naturalization certificate) come back separately by First Class Mail, up to 4 weeks after the passport. If they have not arrived after 4 weeks, call 877-487-2778. To ask for reimbursement of a lost supporting document, contact the State Department within 120 days of the date it mailed the passport, and provide a receipt for the replacement cost. [source]

The child's photo

  • One color photo taken in the last 6 months. Printed size 2 x 2 inches (51 x 51 mm), with the head 1 to 1 3/8 inches (25 to 35 mm) from chin to top of head. Do not attach it yourself. [source, source]
  • Plain white or off-white background, no shadows, no filters or digital edits. No glasses. No uniform. [source]
  • For a baby or toddler, put the child on a plain white or off-white sheet, or cover a car seat with that sheet and sit the child in it. No other person in the photo. The child faces the camera, full face visible, neutral expression, no shadows on the face. [source]
  • A baby's eyes do not have to be entirely open. Every older child must have both eyes open. [source]
  • A photo is not accepted if a pacifier, hands, or anything else blocks part of the face. [source]

Broward Clerk takes photos for $15. Miami-Dade library passport desks do not. [source, source]

Ages 16 and 17

Do not use the under-16 consent rules for a 16 or 17 year old. That applicant uses DS-11 in person for a first adult passport. The passport is valid for 10 years. Show that one legal parent or guardian knows about the application, in any one of these ways: [source]

  • The parent or guardian comes along, shows an acceptable photo ID, signs the DS-11, and you bring a photocopy of that ID.
  • You submit a signed note from the parent or guardian and a photocopy of their ID.
  • You list the parent or guardian in the emergency contact section of the application.
  • You pay with a check in the parent or guardian's name.

If it is not clear that a parent knows, the State Department may ask for a notarized DS-3053 and a photocopy of that parent's ID. If the applicant is enrolled in the Children's Passport Issuance Alert Program, the State Department contacts the parent or guardian who enrolled them before it issues the passport. [source]

Fees at 16 and 17 are the adult DS-11 fees, not the under-16 fees: passport book $130, passport card $30, book and card $160, plus the $35 facility fee. [source, source]
